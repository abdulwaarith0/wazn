Sketch only. The real app animates the letters into place and keeps Arabic letter-joining intact, which is the main technical risk to test in the spec step.

Who it's for

Non-native learners of Arabic who learned it as a second language through madrasah or similar classes, down to teenagers. Today they meet roots and patterns as tables to memorise and rarely see the system behind the words. It works for complete beginners and for students who already know some grammar.

The core loop

Look & feel

Looks like a manuscript
Calm and scholarly: ink on warm paper, calligraphic letters, gentle ornament.
Plays like a game
Clean and modern: letters snapping into place, satisfying feedback, small rewards. Easy for a teenager with no instructions.

Why it matters

So people would understand roots and patterns instantly if they could see it, and also for non-native speakers like I who had to learn the language as a second language through madrasah or other means, and to make the learning easier for more people.

What "working" looks like

Someone opens Wazn, picks the root ك-ت-ب, chooses the فاعِل pattern, and watches the letters fly into the slots to form كاتِب ("writer"). It lands in their collection with a small reward. A few words later a new root unlocks. A pattern that doesn't exist for that root shows as greyed out.

The "oh, that's cool" beat: the letters animating from the root into the pattern while keeping Arabic letter-joining intact.

Build phases · Later

Everything is in this build, in three phases. The app is complete and submittable at the end of each phase, so the deadline (Oct 26) is never at risk.

Phase 1 · The kernel (must ship)
  • About 10 regular (sound) three-letter roots
  • Verb Forms I-IV plus four noun patterns: فاعِل · مَفْعُول · مَفْعَل · فِعالة
  • Only real words, hand-checked by you; non-existent combinations greyed out
  • Explore mode: root + pattern, then the animated word with its English meaning
  • Collection and root unlocking
  • Beginner / Grammar switch (labels and explanations only)
  • Progress saved on the device
Phase 2 · Depth
  • Verb Forms V-X
  • Quiz mode
Phase 3 · Sound and harder roots
  • Audio: your recorded clips, one per pattern first, then per word
  • Weak and doubled roots, where letters change or merge on the way in (ق-و-ل + فَعَلَ = قالَ)
Later · after the hackathon build
  • Accounts or a backend: on-device progress is enough until then
  • Browser text-to-speech: only as a fallback where no recording exists
  • Computer-generated words: only if each is checked against a trusted source first
